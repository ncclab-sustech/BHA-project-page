Beyond Hierarchical Alignment

Distilling teacher diversity for brain visual decoding

A non-invasive brain decoder that learns from four vision models at once, composes their predictions, and reconstructs what a person saw with a frozen image generator. This report covers what works, what does not, and what the learned representations reveal.

Summary

Brain decoders are usually trained to imitate one pretrained vision model. We show that this hidden choice matters, that learning from several teachers at once is better, and that most of the gain comes from a simple place: composing teacher-specific matching scores rather than from any particular readout architecture.

  1. State of the art on both public benchmarks. Within-participant 200-way retrieval reaches 87.0% Raw / 93.9% CSLS-2 Top-1 on THINGS-EEG2[1] (previous best 82.6%) and 55.6% / 68.1% on THINGS-MEG[2] (48.0%). Raw scores beat the best prior method on 8 of 10 EEG participants; CSLS-2 on all 10.
  2. Better reconstructions without touching the generator. PixCorr 0.277, SSIM 0.454 and CLIP identification 0.830 against 0.195 / 0.336 / 0.808 for the strongest prior method, with SDXL-Turbo[3] and IP-Adapter[4] kept frozen. Permuting the predicted conditions drops identification to chance, so the images carry stimulus-specific information.
  3. The gain is composition, not capacity. Composing the shared score with teacher-specific scores adds about 5 points over the shared readout alone and 6 over the pretrained backbone. A linear or MLP head on the same backbone stops 2 to 4 points short, and the readout family itself is not what matters.
  4. Cross-participant transfer is the open problem. On a participant never seen in training the decoder reaches 22.5% Raw / 30.4% CSLS-2 Top-1 on EEG: 45 times chance, but far below within-participant accuracy. Readout specialisation does not help here.
  5. The representations are legible. Teacher-specific readouts share one geometry (RSM correlations 0.71 to 0.91) yet attribute opposite signs to the same image regions; model units are concept-selective; the decoder depends on windows near 100 ms and 220 ms; adapting targets toward the brain improves retrieval but hurts reconstruction.

Results at a glance

Retrieval asks the decoder to pick the image a person saw out of 200 candidates from a single trial average. We report Raw scores (each query ranked independently) and CSLS-2 scores[5] (rescaled with the two nearest unlabeled queries, hence transductive). Prior numbers are as reported by their authors[6–13].

Transfer to a participant never seen in training

In the leave-one-subject-out (LOSO) protocol the model is trained on nine participants and tested on the tenth with no target-participant data in training or model selection. BHA transfers better than any prior method, but the gap to within-participant accuracy is the largest effect in this report.

Reconstruction

Two predictors trained on the frozen brain encoder output the generator's own conditions: a semantic predictor targets the IP-Adapter's image features and a spatial predictor targets the VAE latent[14]. SDXL-Turbo then runs five denoising steps from the predicted latent, with the last quarter unconditioned (α = 0.75). Nothing in the generator is fine-tuned, as in earlier frozen-generator decoders[7, 15, 16].

Richer pretraining of the backbone raises structural fidelity (SSIM 0.448 → 0.459 at α = 0.75) but not CLIP identification (0.841 → 0.831), which is why we report structure and semantics separately rather than as one score.

The idea: one target is not enough

Every brain decoder has to choose what to predict. The usual answer is the embedding of one vision model, typically CLIP[17], followed by nearest-neighbour search or generation[6, 7, 18]. Two things are wrong with that answer. Different vision models encode complementary properties of the same image[19–21], and the brain predicts some of them far better than others. And even a fused target leaves the decoder with a single similarity geometry to match against.

Two short results motivate the design. First, with \(K\) teachers whose errors have variance \(\sigma_T^2\) and pairwise correlation \(\rho_T\), the expected risk of a predictor fitted to the teacher average obeys

\[\sigma_{\mathrm{br}}^2 \le \mathbb{E}\,\mathcal{R}_{\mathrm{vis}}(\hat g_K) \le \sigma_{\mathrm{br}}^2 + \chi_n\Big[\sigma_{\mathrm{br}}^2 + \sigma_T^2\Big(\rho_T + \tfrac{1-\rho_T}{K}\Big)\Big],\]

so the bound is strictly below any single-teacher predictor whenever \(K>1\), and the teacher term shrinks by \(K\) when their errors are uncorrelated. Second, alignment is not generation: for a frozen \(L_G\)-Lipschitz decoder \(G\), an adapted target \(\mathbf{t}=V(\mathbf{e})\) with angular error \(\delta\) and drift \(\Delta_G=\mathbb{E}\|G(\mathbf{t})-G(\mathbf{e})\|^2\),

\[\big|\sqrt{\mathcal{R}_G}-\sqrt{\Delta_G}\big| \le L_G\sqrt{2\delta}.\]

Even perfect alignment to an adapted target leaves \(\mathcal{R}_G=\Delta_G\): whatever helps discrimination can move features away from what the generator expects. BHA therefore keeps separate prediction spaces for retrieval and for reconstruction, and Section 8.6 shows the trade-off empirically.

Model

1

BrainViT encoder

Inputs are \(63\times250\) (EEG) or \(271\times250\) (MEG) windows. A parameter-free sensor mapping places channels on a physical grid; a multiscale feature convolution, a local–global sensor convolution and a residual projection with LayerNorm give a 1,024-d embedding \(\mathbf{h}_i=f_\theta(\mathbf{X}_i,s_i)\). 3.17 M parameters, shared by every task.

2

Stage 1: joint brain–visual adaptation

Teacher features \(\mathbf{e}_{ik}=\phi_k(\mathbf{I}_i)\) from OpenCLIP ViT-H/14[22], DFN (layer 6)[23], MAE (layer 18)[24] and DINOv2 (layer 12)[25] are projected to a common space and fused by a learned head. Encoder and fused target are trained together with a bidirectional CLIP loss, so supervision adapts to what is predictable from brain signals.

\[\mathbf{e}_{i0}=\operatorname{norm}\Big[F\Big(\sum_{k=1}^{K}\operatorname{norm}(W_k\mathbf{e}_{ik})\Big)\Big],\qquad \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{pre}}=\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{BiCLIP}}\big(\operatorname{sim}(\mathbf{h}_i,\mathbf{e}_{j0})/\tau_{\mathrm{pre}}\big).\]
3

Stage 2: teacher-specific prediction and composition

With the backbone frozen, an adapter gives \(\mathbf{h}'_i\). Each teacher group predicts its own space through a base projection, an always-on expert and \(R\) routed residual experts[26, 27]; trainable visual adapters adjust each teacher's targets without touching the teacher. The retrieval score keeps the shared component and adds a convex combination of teacher scores through a learned gate.

\[S_{ij}=s^{0}_{ij}+g_i\sum_{k=1}^{K}\pi_{ik}\,s^{k}_{ij},\qquad \pi_{ik}=\frac{\exp(a_{ik})}{\sum_m \exp(a_{im})},\quad g_i=\sigma(\mathbf{w}_g^\top\mathbf{h}'_i+b_g).\]

Stage 2 trains 10.2 M parameters (adapter 2.1 M, shared readout 0.53 M, group readouts 3.6 M, visual adapters 3.9 M, routing and gate 9 K) on top of 9.2 M frozen ones.

4

Reconstruction without generator fine-tuning

Semantic and spatial predictors are trained separately on the frozen backbone. A mixing parameter \(\alpha\) sets how many of the \(N_{\mathrm{diff}}\) denoising updates run under semantic conditioning, starting from the predicted latent \(\hat{\mathbf{z}}\); optional training-free guidance pulls the clean-latent estimate toward it during sampling. Main-table settings: SDXL-Turbo with IP-Adapter, \(\alpha=0.75\), 5 steps, classifier-free guidance 0, three draws per image.

\[\mathbf{z}^{\mathrm{init}}(\alpha)=\begin{cases}a\hat{\mathbf{z}}, & k_\alpha=0\\ a\hat{\mathbf{z}}+\sigma_{t}\,\boldsymbol{\epsilon}, & 0<k_\alpha<N_{\mathrm{diff}}\\ \sigma_{\mathrm{init}}\boldsymbol{\epsilon}, & k_\alpha=N_{\mathrm{diff}}\end{cases}\qquad \hat{\mathbf{I}}=D_{\mathrm{VAE}}\big(\mathcal{S}_\alpha(\mathbf{z}^{\mathrm{init}}(\alpha);\mathbf{c}_{\mathrm{sem}})/a\big).\]

What drives the gain

Two questions, answered in order: how much each training stage adds, and whether the readout family matters once the backbone is fixed.

The Group-MoE readout used in the paper is one implementation of this composition. In capacity-matched follow-up tests with three seeds, MLP and low-rank linear readouts reach the same accuracy, so we describe the mechanism as composition with learned weights rather than as any particular architecture.

Rescoring and the pretraining target

CSLS-k rescoring helps everywhere and k = 2, selected on validation data, is best within participant; LOSO prefers a larger neighbourhood. For reconstruction, pretraining the backbone against the fused target rather than OpenCLIP alone raises SSIM at every α but leaves CLIP identification unchanged or slightly lower.

Data efficiency

Each fraction of THINGS-EEG2 trains a separate backbone. Composition helps at every scale, and the gap is widest where data is scarcest: with a quarter of the training set, composed scores reach 53.3% against 42.7% for the pretrained backbone alone. A decoder trained on 25% of the data with BHA matches one trained on roughly 50% without it.

Interpretability and analysis

Because BHA keeps one readout per teacher, the model can be asked questions a single-target decoder cannot: which teachers the brain predicts, whether the teacher-specific readouts agree, where in the image each one looks, what the units respond to, and when in time the decision is made. All of these describe the learned model, not neurons in cortex.

8.3 Same geometry, opposite attributions

If the student outputs are so correlated, do the readouts simply do the same thing? No. Occluding image patches and measuring the change in each expert's match score shows that experts with near-identical geometry can disagree about the sign of a region's contribution.

8.4 Concept-selective units

Following CLIP-Dissect[28], each unit of the fused representation is described by the concept whose vision-side activation profile best matches the unit's brain-side profile. Concepts are selected on training images and scored on held-out images.

8.6 Alignment is not generation

The second bound in Section 4 predicts a trade-off, and it appears in practice. Under the BiCLIP objective, adapting the visual target toward the brain makes retrieval better and reconstruction worse at the same time.

Limitations

  • Cross-participant transfer. LOSO accuracy is 22.5% Raw / 30.4% CSLS-2 on EEG and 3.6% / 5.3% on MEG. The encoder itself transfers poorly (22.5% versus 80.8% within), which leaves little teacher-specific signal to compose.
  • Transductive rescoring. CSLS uses the unlabeled query set as references and depends on the cohort. Raw numbers are the non-transductive ones.
  • Metric-dependent reconstruction. Richer pretraining helps structure (SSIM) but not semantic identification at stronger generation settings; no single number summarises reconstruction quality.
  • Temporal interpretation. The 220 ms sensitivity overlaps image offset and the next stimulus; masking cannot separate these.
  • Interpretability claims are about the model. Concept-selective units and shared geometry describe the learned representations; they do not establish corresponding neurons or causal mechanisms in cortex.

Setup and reproducibility

Data
THINGS-EEG2: 10 participants, 63 channels, 250 Hz, 1,654 concepts × 10 images for training (16,540 images), 200 held-out test images, 80 repetitions per test image. THINGS-MEG: 4 participants, 271 channels. Reconstruction is evaluated on THINGS-EEG2.
Teachers
OpenCLIP ViT-H/14, DFN (layer 6), MAE (layer 18), DINOv2 (layer 12). Layers were chosen by single-teacher retrieval; intermediate layers of self-supervised models are more brain-predictable than their final layers[29].
Protocols
Within participant: train and test on the same person. LOSO: train on all others, test on the held-out person. Raw ranks each query independently; CSLS-k rescales scores with the k nearest unlabeled queries (k = 2 selected on validation).
Metrics
200-way Top-1 / Top-5 retrieval. Reconstruction: PixCorr, SSIM, AlexNet(2/5), InceptionV3 and CLIP two-way identification (higher is better); EfficientNet-B and SwAV distances (lower is better).
Seeds
Main results use seed 42 with per-participant models; every follow-up test in Section 6 uses seeds 42, 43 and 44 with the validation split fixed and checkpoint selection on validation Raw Top-1.
Generation
SDXL-Turbo with IP-Adapter, ip scale 1.5, α = 0.75, 5 steps, classifier-free guidance 0, three draws per image. Optional VAE-guided variant reported separately.
Release
Code, configs, pretrained weights and per-participant tables will be released after review. Prior-method numbers are as reported in their papers. This page is anonymous while the manuscript is under review.

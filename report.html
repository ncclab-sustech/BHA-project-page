Beyond Hierarchical Alignment
Distilling teacher diversity for brain visual decoding

How BHA learns one brain representation from four vision teachers, composes their predictions for retrieval, reconstructs images with a frozen generator, and what the learned representations reveal. Written for practitioners; every number is from the manuscript or from pre-registered follow-up experiments.

01TL;DR

  • Problem. Brain decoders predict the features of a pretrained vision model, then search or generate. Which model to imitate is a hidden design choice: different vision models capture complementary stimulus properties, and the brain predicts some far better than others. Fusing them into one target still leaves a single similarity geometry.
  • Method. BHA jointly learns a brain encoder (BrainViT) and a fused visual target from four teachers (OpenCLIP ViT-H/14, DFN-6, MAE-18, DINOv2-12). It then freezes the backbone and trains teacher-specific readouts whose matching scores are composed with the shared score by learned weights. For reconstruction, separate semantic and spatial adapters predict the generator’s native conditions; the generator stays frozen.
  • Results. Within-subject 200-way retrieval: 87.0% Raw / 93.9% CSLS-2 on THINGS-EEG2 (previous best 82.6%), 55.6% / 68.1% on THINGS-MEG (48.0%). Reconstruction: PixCorr 0.277, SSIM 0.454, CLIP 0.830 vs. 0.195 / 0.336 / 0.808 for the strongest prior method. Gains hold at 25% of the training data.
  • What is new, honestly. The gain comes from composing teacher-specific scores, not from the readout’s capacity: pre-registered tests show linear, MLP and Group-MoE readouts reach the same accuracy. Cross-subject transfer remains limited (22.5% Raw / 30.4% CSLS-2 on EEG).
  • Analysis. Teacher-specific readouts share one geometry (RSM correlations 0.71–0.91) yet attribute opposite signs to the same image regions; model units are concept-selective; the decoder depends on 100 ms and 220 ms windows; adapting targets toward the brain improves retrieval but hurts reconstruction.

02Why one target is not enough

Let \(\mathbf{b}\) be brain features and \(\mathbf{y}=\mathbf{M}_\star\mathbf{b}+\boldsymbol{\xi}\) the visual target. Teachers provide noisy views \(\mathbf{t}_k=\mathbf{y}+\boldsymbol{\delta}_k\) with per-teacher error \(\sigma_T^2\) and pairwise correlation \(\rho_T\). Fitting a least-squares predictor to the teacher average gives

\[\sigma_{\mathrm{br}}^2 \le \mathbb{E}\,\mathcal{R}_{\mathrm{vis}}(\hat g_K) \le \sigma_{\mathrm{br}}^2 + \chi_n\Big[\sigma_{\mathrm{br}}^2 + \sigma_T^2\Big(\rho_T + \tfrac{1-\rho_T}{K}\Big)\Big].\]

With \(K>1\) the bound is strictly below any single-teacher predictor; with uncorrelated teacher errors the teacher term shrinks by \(K\). This motivates learning from several teachers, but says nothing about which space to predict in. A second result separates alignment from generation: for a frozen \(L_G\)-Lipschitz decoder \(G\), an adapted target \(\mathbf{t}=V(\mathbf{e})\), angular error \(\delta\) and adaptation drift \(\Delta_G=\mathbb{E}\|G(\mathbf{t})-G(\mathbf{e})\|^2\),

\[\big|\sqrt{\mathcal{R}_G}-\sqrt{\Delta_G}\big| \le L_G\sqrt{2\delta}.\]

Even perfect alignment to an adapted target leaves \(\mathcal{R}_G=\Delta_G\): what helps discrimination can move the features away from what the generator expects. BHA therefore keeps one prediction space per task.

03Model

3.1 BrainViT encoder

Inputs are \(63\times250\) (EEG) or \(271\times250\) (MEG) windows. A parameter-free sensor mapping places channels on a physical grid, a multiscale feature convolution with pooling extracts \(64\times63\times36\) features, a local–global sensor convolution with pointwise projection reduces to \(40\times36\), and a residual projection with LayerNorm yields a 1,024-d embedding \(\mathbf{h}_i=f_\theta(\mathbf{X}_i,s_i)\). Total: 3.17 M parameters. Among five readouts searched, local–global sensor convolution gave the best within-subject accuracy (83.1 ± 5.4% Raw Top-1); the main results use the default feature–sensor readout (80.9 ± 7.0%).

3.2 Stage 1 — joint brain–visual adaptation

Teacher features \(\mathbf{e}_{ik}=\phi_k(\mathbf{I}_i)\) are projected to a common space and fused by a learned \(F\):

\[\mathbf{e}_{i0}=\operatorname{norm}\Big[F\Big(\sum_{k=1}^{K}\operatorname{norm}(W_k\mathbf{e}_{ik})\Big)\Big],\qquad \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{pre}}=\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{BiCLIP}}\big(\operatorname{sim}(\mathbf{h}_i,\mathbf{e}_{j0})/\tau_{\mathrm{pre}}\big).\]

The encoder and the fused target are learned together, so supervision adapts to what is predictable from brain signals.

3.3 Stage 2 — teacher-specific prediction and composition

With BrainViT and fusion frozen, a retrieval adapter gives \(\mathbf{h}'_i=A(\operatorname{sg}(\mathbf{h}_i))\). Each teacher group \(k\) predicts its own space through a base projection, an always-on expert and \(R\) routed residual experts:

\[\hat{\mathbf{e}}_{ik}=P_k\mathbf{h}'_i+U_k(\mathbf{h}'_i)+\sum_{r=1}^{R} w_{ikr}E_{kr}(\mathbf{h}'_i),\qquad \ell^{k}_{ij}=\operatorname{sim}(\hat{\mathbf{e}}_{ik},\tilde{\mathbf{e}}_{jk})/\tau_k.\]

Trainable visual adapters \(\tilde{\mathbf{e}}_{ik}=V_k(\operatorname{sg}(\mathbf{e}_{ik}))\) adjust each teacher’s targets without touching the teacher. The composed retrieval score keeps the shared component and adds a convex combination of teacher scores with a learned gate:

\[S_{ij}=s^{0}_{ij}+g_i\sum_{k=1}^{K}\pi_{ik}\,s^{k}_{ij},\qquad \pi_{ik}=\frac{\exp(a_{ik})}{\sum_m \exp(a_{im})},\quad g_i=\sigma(\mathbf{w}_g^\top\mathbf{h}'_i+b_g).\]

Stage 2 trains 10.2 M parameters (adapter 2.1 M, shared readout 0.53 M, group readouts 3.6 M, visual adapters 3.9 M, routing and gate 9 K) on top of 9.2 M frozen ones. Section 7 shows the choice of readout family does not matter for accuracy.

3.4 Reconstruction without generator fine-tuning

Semantic and spatial predictors are trained separately on the frozen backbone to output the generator’s own conditioning features and VAE latents. A mixing parameter \(\alpha\in[0,1]\) sets how many of the \(N_{\mathrm{diff}}\) denoising updates run under semantic conditioning, starting from the predicted latent \(\hat{\mathbf{z}}\):

\[\mathbf{z}^{\mathrm{init}}(\alpha)=\begin{cases}a\hat{\mathbf{z}}, & k_\alpha=0\\ a\hat{\mathbf{z}}+\sigma_{t}\,\boldsymbol{\epsilon}, & 0<k_\alpha<N_{\mathrm{diff}}\\ \sigma_{\mathrm{init}}\boldsymbol{\epsilon}, & k_\alpha=N_{\mathrm{diff}}\end{cases}\qquad \hat{\mathbf{I}}=D_{\mathrm{VAE}}\big(\mathcal{S}_\alpha(\mathbf{z}^{\mathrm{init}}(\alpha);\mathbf{c}_{\mathrm{sem}})/a\big).\]

Optional training-free guidance pulls the estimated clean latent toward the brain-predicted latent during sampling. Main-table settings: SDXL-Turbo with IP-Adapter, \(\alpha=0.75\), 5 steps, classifier-free guidance 0, three draws per image.

04Setup

Data

THINGS-EEG2: 10 participants, 63 channels, 250 Hz, 1,654 concepts × 10 images for training (16,540), 200 held-out test images, 80 repetitions per test image. THINGS-MEG: 4 participants, 271 channels. Image reconstruction is evaluated on THINGS-EEG2.

Teachers

OpenCLIP ViT-H/14, DFN (layer 6), MAE (layer 18), DINOv2 (layer 12). Layers were chosen by single-teacher retrieval; intermediate layers of self-supervised models are more brain-predictable than their final layers.

Protocols

Within-subject: train and test on the same participant. LOSO: train on all others, test on the held-out participant. Raw scores rank each query independently; CSLS-\(k\) rescales scores with the \(k\) nearest unlabeled queries and is therefore transductive (\(k=2\) selected on validation data).

Metrics

200-way Top-1 / Top-5 retrieval. Reconstruction: PixCorr, SSIM, AlexNet(2/5), InceptionV3, CLIP two-way identification (higher is better); EfficientNet-B and SwAV distances (lower is better). Three seeds for all follow-up tests.

07What the readout does — and doesn’t

Reviewers asked whether the Group-MoE readout is doing the work. We pre-registered four tests before running them (frozen backbone, 10 participants × 3 seeds, decision rule: MoE “helps” only if it beats both capacity-matched baselines by ≥ 0.5 points and wins ≥ 20 of 30 subject–seed pairs). All results are reported regardless of outcome.

Composed Raw Top-1 (%) at equal readout parameter budgets (expert rank r = 4 … 256). Group MoE, MLP and low-rank linear readouts are indistinguishable at every budget; the paper setting is r = 64.

Parameter budget

At r = 4 / 16 / 64 / 256 the composed score reaches 86.8 / 86.5 / 86.5 / 86.7 (MoE), 86.5 / 86.6 / 86.6 / 85.9 (MLP) and 86.7 / 86.6 / 86.5 / 86.4 (linear). Largest MoE margin: 0.3 points. Verdict: not more parameter-efficient.

Stronger encoder

With the local–global sensor-convolution encoder (Stage 1: 81.5 vs 81.1), the three readouts reach 87.6 / 87.6 / 87.6. Composition gain over the shared expert: +4.3 points for all three.

Pooled model

One model trained on all ten participants, evaluated per participant: 89.6 (MoE) / 89.4 (MLP) / 89.9 (linear).

Conclusion

The gain is in composing teacher-specific scores with the shared score, not in the readout family. We now describe the mechanism as composition with learned weights; the MoE is an implementation choice.

08Interpretability and analysis

8.1 Which teachers are brain-predictable

Paired brain–image cosine on THINGS-EEG2 test images, ten-participant means. Raw: OpenCLIP 0.204, DFN-6 0.256, MAE-18 0.041, DINOv2-12 0.050. Adapted: 0.399 / 0.611 / 0.545 / 0.474. Shared target: 0.624. Non-paired baseline ≈ 0.

8.2 Shared geometry across teacher-specific outputs

Pearson correlation between representational similarity matrices, ten-participant means. Teachers: 0.07–0.70. Student outputs predicted from the same brain signal: 0.71–0.91. The shared expert best preserves inter-image relations (r = 0.82).

8.3 Opposite attributions on the same regions

Occlusion attribution of each expert’s match score (ten-participant mean maps, 16×16 patches, signed within-map percentiles). For the bonnet, OpenCLIP and DFN experts raise the match from the hat while MAE and DINOv2 experts lower it from the same pixels. Similar embedding geometry does not imply interchangeable matching functions.

8.4 Concept selectivity

CLIP-Dissect associates model units with 27 THINGS categories; concepts are selected on training images and evaluated on held-out images. Brain–visual concept-profile cosine: OpenCLIP 0.612, DFN-6 0.726, MAE-18 0.796, DINOv2-12 0.812, shared 0.814; neuron shuffling ≈ 0. These characterise the learned model, not neuron identities in cortex.

8.5 Temporal dependence

50 ms windows, 10 ms stride; frozen models. Left: Top-1 lost when the window is deleted. Right: Top-1 when only the window is kept (chance 0.5%). Sensitivity peaks near 100–125 ms and again around 220 ms; later peaks overlap image offset, so masking cannot separate those contributions.

8.6 Alignment is not generation

Under the BiCLIP objective, adapting the visual target toward the brain raises paired cosine from 0.217 to 0.481 and Raw Top-1 from 30.05% to 37.80%, but lowers CLIP identification of reconstructions from 0.754 to 0.706. Under an MSE–BiCLIP objective, adaptation lowers both. This is the empirical face of the second bound in Section 2 and the reason reconstruction uses generator-native prediction spaces.

09Limitations

  • Cross-subject transfer. LOSO accuracy is 22.5% Raw / 30.4% CSLS-2 on EEG and 3.6% / 5.3% on MEG. The encoder itself transfers poorly (22.5% vs 80.8% within), leaving little teacher-specific signal to compose; readout specialization does not help here.
  • Transductive rescoring. CSLS uses the unlabeled query set as references and depends on the cohort; Raw numbers are the non-transductive ones.
  • Reconstruction is metric-dependent. Richer pretraining helps structure (SSIM) but not semantic identification at stronger generation settings.
  • Temporal interpretation. The 220 ms sensitivity overlaps image offset and the next stimulus; masking cannot separate these.
  • Interpretability claims are about the model. Concept-selective units and shared geometry describe the learned representations; they do not establish corresponding neurons or causal mechanisms in cortex.

10Reproducibility

Main results use seed 42 with subject-wise models; every follow-up test in Section 7 uses seeds 42, 43 and 44 with the validation split fixed to seed 42 and checkpoint selection on validation Raw Top-1. Stage 2 keeps BrainViT and fusion frozen; the unfrozen variant was also tested as a pre-registered final scenario. Reconstruction uses SDXL-Turbo with IP-Adapter (ip scale 1.5, α = 0.75, 5 steps, CFG 0, three draws). Code, configs and pretrained weights will be released after review together with the per-subject tables.

Prior-method numbers are as reported in their papers. This page is kept anonymous while the manuscript is under review.
